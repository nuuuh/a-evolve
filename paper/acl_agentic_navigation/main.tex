\title{Agentic Evolution under Long-Horizon, Non-Stationary, Open-Ended Task Streams:\\
A Regret Decomposition and Three Composable Architectural Remedies}

\author{Anonymous ACL submission}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Self-evolving agents are typically evaluated on i.i.d.\ benchmarks that omit three properties of real-world deployment: \textbf{long-horizon} (D1), \textbf{non-stationarity} (D2), and \textbf{open-endedness} (D3). On three temporal benchmarks that exercise all three --- PolyBench, CTF-Dojo, FutureX --- we characterise three task-intrinsic challenges: \textbf{C1} \emph{non-monotonic evolution} (early-freeze beats full-evo under shift); \textbf{C2} \emph{construction bottleneck} (prompt-level evolvers cannot build multi-file infrastructure); \textbf{C3} \emph{experience insufficiency} (under severe shift, history carries no signal). We partition regret as \emph{evolution loss} $\Levo(\Phi, K)$ (what the evolver class can construct at capacity~$K$) plus \emph{navigation loss} $\Lnav(\varphi)$ (whether the harness is conditioned on the task); experience availability sits outside the decomposition as a third axis. Three composable architectural contributions follow: agentic navigation via a git-backed strategy tree conditions the harness per task and reduces $\Lnav$; multi-agent evolution with persistent cross-cycle state expands the constructible harness class and reduces $\Levo$; and human-in-the-loop integration supplies external direction when history is insufficient. Each is independently effective in ablation, and together they close most of the gap to a hand-designed expert reference on all three benchmarks.
\end{abstract}
